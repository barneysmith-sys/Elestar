01 - The Circuit

Every hire is an elimination round.
We list the ones who almost survived it.

Most candidates who clear three or more rounds don't get an offer - not because they weren't good, but because one seat existed and someone else took it.

01

Applied

Resume submitted through the hiring company's own process.

02

Screened

Passed recruiter screen or assessment. Roughly 1 in 4 applicants get here.

03

Semifinalist

Advanced to onsite or panel rounds - real interviewer hours spent.

04

Finalist

Reached the final round: committee, partner, or offer-stage conversation.

05

Listed

No offer, or an offer taken elsewhere. Evidence gathered and added to elestar at the round reached.

For companies

Round five is where sourcing gets cheaper - you're not paying interviewers to re-run rounds one through four.

For candidates

Round five is where the work you already did stops going to waste the moment one rejection email arrives.

04 - Verification

Confidence has levels. We show all of them.

A badge that just says "Verified" hides more than it reveals. Every dossier shows exactly how its evidence was established.

On a dossier, this shows as: Corroborated
01

Read the record, don't accept a copy. Email and calendar connections let us check sender domains and message headers directly - far harder to fake than an uploaded screenshot.

02

Require agreement across sources. No single document moves a dossier past Level 01. We look for independent signals that corroborate each other.

03

Ask the company, briefly. A one-click confirmation request goes to the recruiter's verified address. Most go unanswered - the ones that come back matter a lot.

04

Real identity behind an anonymous listing. Candidates verify who they are at signup. A caught fraud costs a real identity, not a throwaway account.

05 - Where AI Does the Work

Three jobs that don't scale by hand.

None of this is a chat widget bolted onto the page. Each piece replaces a manual task that stops working once the pool gets past a few hundred dossiers.

Verification

Pattern review across the pool

Flags claims that don't match a company's typical process, documents reused across different candidate accounts, and clusters of correlated claims worth a manual look.

→ Fraud checks that don't scale one dossier at a time
Sourcing

Matching, not browsing

A recruiter describes the role; matching surfaces finalists whose cleared rounds actually tested the same skills - including candidates whose title doesn't match on paper.

→ Replaces scrolling a dossier list by hand
Interviewing

Skip-ahead briefs

Before a first call, the hiring team gets a summary of exactly what was already tested elsewhere - system design, case, behavioral - and what's still worth probing.

→ Turns "start over" into "pick up where it left off"
06 - Before You Ask

Who can see my name?

No one, until you approve a specific introduction. Companies browse anonymized dossiers - tier, function, rounds cleared, evidence level - with identity withheld.

Does listing cost anything?

No. Listing is free for candidates for as long as you're on elestar. Companies pay for the access tier that lets them browse and request intros.

List a process

Describe it. The agent does the rest.

No form. Write what you went through the way you'd tell a friend, and the pipeline below turns it into a structured, anonymous record.

Rounds you completed — not how far you were told you'd get. Don't include your name or your employer's; anything identifying is stripped before the model sees a word of it.

Pipeline

·Redact identity
·Parse the process
·Compute tier (deterministic)
·Re-identification audit
·Publish to the circuit

Why five steps

Redaction runs first and always — if it fails, nothing proceeds. The model proposes a tier but a deterministic rule decides it, and that rule can lower the proposal, never raise it. Brand is not an input: a famous company with one screening call is Standard.

The audit checks how many other live records share your combination of tier, function and region. Below eight, detail gets generalized until it clears. Every step fails closed.

Search the pool

Describe the role, not the keywords.

Ranked by whether the competencies already assessed overlap with what this role needs — never by employer prestige, school, or years of experience.

Your floor: Filters run in code before the model sees anything

Ranking

·Apply hard filters
·Rank by competency overlap
·Write rationales

Why filters run first

Your tier and evidence floors are enforced in code, never passed to the model as an instruction. A model that can be talked out of a floor can be talked out of a blocklist, and that is a privacy incident rather than a ranking bug.

Every row names a gap

A rationale with no negative in it is a sales pitch, and it teaches recruiters to stop reading rationales. If the model returns one without a gap, it's rejected.
