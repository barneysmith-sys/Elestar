# Interview Brief

The moment the product delivers on its promise. A recruiter has requested an
intro, the candidate has approved, and this is what lands in their inbox.

If the brief is generic, the recruiter runs their normal five-round loop anyway,
and elestar was an expensive sourcing tool.

## Structure

Four sections, in this order, no more than one page:

1. **Already assessed** - competency, depth, and the format it was assessed in.
   This is the evidence, and it comes first because it is what justifies
   everything below.
2. **Safe to skip** - the specific rounds this team can drop, each with the
   assessment it is redundant with. Name the round type as this team calls it.
3. **Probe instead** - what was *not* covered. Usually domain specifics, the
   team's stack, and anything the previous loop passed over.
4. **Context on the outcome** - why the previous process ended, stated plainly
   and without spin.

## Say what to skip, specifically

"They have been technically assessed" is useless. "Their five-round loop included
a dedicated system design round on a rate-limiting problem and a Go coding round
- your technical screen and system design round are both redundant" is the
product.

Never recommend skipping more than half a team's loop. Beyond that recruiters
stop trusting the brief, and there is a real limit to what another company's
process tells you about fit for yours. Two rounds skipped from a five-round loop
is a credible, valuable claim. Four is a claim nobody will act on.

## Never skip these, and say so

- Team fit and working-style rounds. Not transferable in principle.
- Anything domain-specific to the hiring company.
- Anything with a regulatory or compliance requirement.
- The final decision-maker conversation.

Stating the limits is what makes the rest of the brief credible.

## On the previous outcome

Report the category from the record without softening or dramatising it. If it
was `req_closed`, say so - it is the most common case and it carries no negative
signal. If it was `performance`, say that too; concealing it would make every
other brief suspect, and a recruiter who finds out later stops using the product.

If the outcome is `unstated`, write "the candidate did not state a reason."
Do not speculate, and do not fill the space.

## Tone

Written to a hiring manager who is busy and slightly sceptical. No adjectives
about the candidate. No sales language. Every sentence should be a fact plus its
source, or a recommendation plus its reason.
